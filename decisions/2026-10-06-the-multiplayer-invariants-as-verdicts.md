# The multiplayer invariants, as verdicts

- Question: PRACTICES names four multiplayer invariants and the brief asks for an `always!` each, plus every view catching up within the coalescing interval. How are they judged, and what does "within the coalescing interval" allow for the network?
- Call: nine invariants (`testbeds/multiplayer/README.md`): PRACTICES' four, split where a failure means different things (applied once; acknowledged and visible in the next snapshot and at its receipt's revision; nothing unacknowledged applied; nothing surfaced applied; only conflicts refused; gap-free revisions; replay equals state), plus H5's safety (a patch resubmitted on its own lands over no commit touching what it touches) and H6's catch-up. The truth is read through the service after writes stop, not over the faulted network. Each broken invariant is a `violation` verdict and the run exits 1, not an `always!`, because an `always!` abort leaves no trace to replay or minimize (patina gap 6). Catch-up: every view holds the store's revisions within 450 ms after the last write is answered, the 250 ms coalescing interval plus one 200 ms attempt for the tick and the refetch to cross the network.
- Alternatives: `always!` as the brief says (failures unreplayable); a catch-up deadline of the coalescing interval alone (fails on network delay the sweep injects on purpose).
- What would change it: patina finalizing a trace on an `always!` abort; a view needing more than one attempt to catch up under the sweep's faults (the slowest so far took about 280 ms).
