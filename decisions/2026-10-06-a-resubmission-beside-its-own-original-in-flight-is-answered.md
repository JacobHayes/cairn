# A resubmission beside its own original in flight is answered stale (product bug, fixed)

- Question: the multiplayer testbed's smoke campaign failed about one generation in eight (8 of 64) on `nothing-unacknowledged-applied` and `surfaced-not-applied`: a patch its caller was told conflicted is in the journey. Is that the testbed or the product, and how is it fixed?
- Finding (6.1): the product (H5). Mechanism, from the minimized seed (`briefs/proof/6.1/README.md`): a commit holds its transaction open past the caller's attempt timeout; the caller resubmits the same patch; its commit conflicts twice with the original's revision row and, finding no receipt yet, the store answered stale naming the revision in flight (base plus one) with nothing intervening (3.1's call for a twice-conflicted commit). The client's retry (4.2) rebased onto that revision; the engine answered that the patch names a revision past the current one; the client rebased back; the 32-resubmission bound ran out in no virtual time and the conflict was surfaced; then the original landed. The same answer given to any other patch beside a commit in flight named that revision with nothing intervening even when the commit in flight touched what the patch did, so the client could take an unsafe retry as safe. Since 4.2's review a patch runs on its own task, so an HTTP resubmission can meet its original too, not only the in-process agent.
- Call (the fix): (1) the Turso store queues commits in process on what they write: before it begins, a commit takes its turn at every revision row it writes (its target's, its dependencies', the deployment's when it writes it) and at its patch id, in one order, holding them until its transaction ends and waiting at most `store_connection_acquire` (then `AcquireTimeout`, a 503). A resubmission beside its original waits for it and finds its receipt inside the transaction (`AlreadyApplied`); any other commit beside one in flight waits and is then checked against what really committed, so a stale answer carries the real intervening touched set. Commits to different domains wait on each other only through a revision row they both write: a commit that writes an entity reference (a node, answer, role fill, or participation naming an entity), or creates an entity, also claims the deployment's revision row, so those commits queue behind each other across every journey and route (and beside deployment patches and proposals to the deployment); before the fix they already conflicted there, and the second lost or got the phantom answer. A commit held near the acquire limit on one journey can therefore make entity-referencing commits to other journeys answer 503. (2) A second write-write conflict, now possible only from a connection outside those turns, is answered stale only with revisions that have really moved; when none has, it fails rather than name a revision still in flight. (3) The client's safe retry never rebases backward: a conflict whose current revision is not past the one the patch named is surfaced, so no pair of answers can send a patch back and forth. The memory store, the browser host's included, commits whole under one lock, so nothing is ever in flight beside a commit there and it needs no change. Tests: `crates/service/tests/in_flight.rs` (both cases over both stores; with the turns removed the Turso cases fail with `conflicted twice with a commit still in flight`), Turso's race cases (now showing the second commit waits and what it then gets), `client::retry`'s `submit_never_rebases_backward`; the smoke campaign passes 16 of 16 generations with no tolerated finding.
- Alternatives: per-patch-id in-flight tracking in the service (fixes the resubmission only; any other patch beside a commit in flight still got the empty stale answer); answering a twice-conflicted commit as a retryable failure (the first test's caller still gets no receipt, and every commit beside a slow one fails); waiting for "some commit to end" and retrying (two commits holding each other's rows could each wait for the other); the client alone refusing backward rebases (stops the ping-pong but still surfaces a conflict for a patch that lands); and, from the first call, reporting the revision the commit read (then `expected` equals `current`, which reads as no conflict) or retrying with a backoff (a knob, and commits take milliseconds).
- What would change it: a second server process on one database (the turns are per process; ARCHITECTURE assumes one); a commit beside a slow one waiting near the acquire limit in practice; or the deployment-wide queue of entity-referencing commits showing up as latency, which would call for narrowing the deployment claim (to commits that create entities, with a merge's referencing check carried another way), a design change needing its own decision.
- History: the store's first call (3.1, "a commit that conflicts twice is answered stale with the revision in flight") retried a Turso write-write conflict once and answered a second conflict `stale` naming the revision the in-flight commit was producing (the base plus one), leaving the client's refetch-and-resubmit as the retry; the multiplayer testbed's finding above showed that answer is what sent a resubmission back and forth, so this fix replaced it.
