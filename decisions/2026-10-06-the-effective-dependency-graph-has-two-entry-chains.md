# The effective dependency graph has two entry chains, a structural full set, and a pruned set with pass-through entries

- Question: ARCHITECTURE has condition gates travel the entry chain apart from requirements and stop at a force-included node, which drops its ancestors' conditions but keeps their requirements; one entry per container cannot carry both. It also leaves open whether the full set (invariants, tracing) honors force include, and how pruning keeps a force-included node under a not-relevant container waiting on that container's requirements.
- Call: a container has two entries, a requirement entry (explicit requirements and stage openings wait there) and a condition entry (condition gates wait there); a child's requirement anchor waits on the parent's requirement entry and its condition anchor on the parent's condition entry. A non-container's start is both anchors. The full set is structural: every edge the graph's structure makes, whatever relevance or overrides say, so the cycle check holds routes and journeys alike and a force include cannot hide a cycle that removing it would expose. The pruned set leaves out every edge that waits on a not-relevant node or holds one's own work, except edges into a requirement entry, which stay as a pass-through, and the condition gates and condition-chain links into a force-included node. Every edge is a gate except a stage opening with `gates: false`, which is date-only; the cycle check (now built on this graph, replacing 2.1's network) reads gate edges only, so it covers explicit edges and every implicit gate the acyclicity invariant names (containment, inherited requirements, condition gates, stage openings). Edges to self, ancestors, and descendants are reported under their own codes and left out of the cycle check, so one cause gives one violation. A node's effective dependencies are listed on demand by walking its entry chains, never stored per descendant. 2.3 and 2.4 read the same graph and the same split.
- Alternatives: edge-class filtering on one chain (condition and requirement edges into one entry cannot be told apart once merged); a full set that honors force include (an override could then make a structural cycle legal until removed); copying inherited requirements onto descendants (up to nodes x depth x 64 edges).
- What would change it: the date network (2.3) needing entry instants of its own beyond these two, or blocking (2.4) needing a precomputed per-node list that the chain walk cannot give in time.
- History: the engine's first cut (2.1, "the cycle check covers inherited requirements; condition gates and stages join with 2.2") checked cycles over an instant network of explicit requirements and parent-child waits, so a cycle through an inherited requirement was caught before relevance existed, and left condition gates and stage openings to 2.2; that record expected the check to move if 2.2 built the effective graph as a separate structure, which this call does.
