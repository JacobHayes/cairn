# The effective dependency graph has two entry chains, a structural full set, and a pruned set with pass-through entries

- Question: ARCHITECTURE has condition gates travel the entry chain apart from requirements and stop at a force-included node, which drops its ancestors' conditions but keeps their requirements; one entry per container cannot carry both. It also leaves open whether the full set (invariants, tracing) honors force include, and how pruning keeps a force-included node under a not-relevant container waiting on that container's requirements.
- Call: a container has two entries, a requirement entry (explicit requirements and stage openings wait there) and a condition entry (condition gates wait there); a child's requirement anchor waits on the parent's requirement entry and its condition anchor on the parent's condition entry. A non-container's start is both anchors. The full set is structural: every edge the graph's structure makes, whatever relevance or overrides say, so the cycle check holds routes and journeys alike and a force include cannot hide a cycle that removing it would expose. The pruned set leaves out every edge that waits on a not-relevant node or holds one's own work, except edges into a requirement entry, which stay as a pass-through, and the condition gates and condition-chain links into a force-included node. Every edge is a gate except a stage opening with `gates: false`, which is date-only; the cycle check (now built on this graph, replacing 2.1's network) reads gate edges only. A node's effective dependencies are listed on demand by walking its entry chains, never stored per descendant. 2.3 and 2.4 read the same graph and the same split.
- Alternatives: edge-class filtering on one chain (condition and requirement edges into one entry cannot be told apart once merged); a full set that honors force include (an override could then make a structural cycle legal until removed); copying inherited requirements onto descendants (up to nodes x depth x 64 edges).
- What would change it: the date network (2.3) needing entry instants of its own beyond these two, or blocking (2.4) needing a precomputed per-node list that the chain walk cannot give in time.
