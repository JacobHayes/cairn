# The chip's timing, the toast, receipts and conversations are actors, and what stays hand-made

Question (from finishing the frontend state work: the timed parts of the shell, the assistant conversation, and the sweep for what the earlier steps left): the sync chip, the toast, a receipt, the chip's popover and the assistant conversation each kept their own timer, flag or generation counter beside a hand-written store with listeners. Which of them become actors, who owns each, and what is left standing on purpose?

Call:

- **The sync scheduler holds only the facts that carry a deadline.** Its context is the lag the shown reads report (the worst over a changing set of reads, which only the session's feed follows), the start of the writes in flight, and the last save's time, with the monotonic time of its last event or wake. It wakes at the next threshold with a delayed transition (today's `min(due)`) and never schedules without a deadline. The rest of what the chip says is read from its owner when it renders: the stream (subscription), skew (root), the network (the browser), problems and the count of writes in flight (edit manager), the revision (journey manager), the host kind. This reads the ticket's "holds the reported facts" narrowly, because the rule is one owner per fact and no pushed copy. The eleven outcomes stay a projection (`summarize`, returning a tagged case that carries each state's data, and `syncWords` for label, tone, sentence and announcement); words and colours are unchanged.
- **`inFlight` and `inFlightSince` are one fact** in the projection (`{ count, since }`): the edit manager owns the count, the scheduler the start of the episode, and `syncInputOf` is the one place they meet. The root relays the count from each manager snapshot, and an event that changes no fact takes no transition, so a relay does not re-render the chip.
- **The write-start count stays a plain sequence on the session** (`Session.begin`). A write's `begin` action needs the count synchronously, and an actor answers only by events; the triage pass reads `Session.began` once at mount. Monotonic time cannot replace it (two writes in one tick are unordered).
- **The recent saves are plain data in the root's context**, added by one root event with a pure `recorded`, which also tells the scheduler the time. A one-state machine for them would be a store with ceremony.
- **The toast is a root child; a receipt and the chip's popover belong to the component that shows them.** A toast is tab-wide (the session's notes raise them). A receipt belongs to the control under which it shows and has nowhere to show once that control is gone; the popover belongs to the one chip. Both are `useActorRef` machines, so they end with their component, which reads the parent's rule that components own their transient actors; a root receipt manager keyed per control would have the same lifetime with a map and two events more. The toast's expiry pauses while the pointer is on it and restarts in full when it leaves; a toast shown meanwhile replaces the old and, if the pointer holds the old, is held too. The popover is two regions, pinned and peeked, so unpinning with the pointer still on the chip leaves it peeked as before; the leave delay is a delayed transition, and the document listeners and focus moves stay in the component, which sends `close`.
- **Every turn ends with a read, so a refresh during a turn waits for that read.** In `sending` and `rereading` a refresh is not taken; a turn answered ends in `rereading`, one that failed in `reading`, so a panel reopened during a turn gets a fresh read once the turn ends. A read in flight when a turn starts is stopped by leaving `reading`. That is the whole guarantee that a read begun before an answer never replaces it, with no counter. The visible difference is small: reopening the panel mid-turn shows the conversation as held, with the working line, until the turn ends, and a failed turn is followed by one more read.
- **"A turn answered after the conversation changes is ignored"** means the conversation that sent it stopped (the session closed): a stopped actor hears nothing, so the answer changes and keeps nothing. Closing the panel or moving it to another target is not a change of conversation: a turn on one target answered after the panel moved on lands in that target's conversation, and opening it again shows the answer.
- **The conversation manager is an explicit keyed map, unbounded per tab, as before.** Each target a person opens adds one conversation, and a conversation keeps at most the turns PRACTICES allows. No limit is added; if tabs that open very many journeys' assistants become a cost, evicting the least recently opened is the wish to record.
- **`useFrame` loses its `close`, `closable` and `toggle` refs.** The close address is state and the key listener's effect depends on what it reads, so re-registering costs nothing. `useAxis` already derives from its keyed override and is unchanged.

What stays hand-made, with its reason: `setTimeout(…, 0)` in `routes/model.ts` frees a download's object URL once the browser took it (no state); `requestAnimationFrame` in the canvas, the row actions' focus wait and the frame waits a frame for layout (no state; the focus wait is bounded by frames); the in-browser host's tick batching in `wasm/src/root.ts` is the server side of that host, not app state; `ui/theme.ts`, `detail/folds.ts`, the frame's `useMedia` and `useOnline` are preferences and browser sources; the canvas's one-shot first fit and reveal markers and its lasso flag guard React Flow callbacks; the context-menu choice, the proposal editor's written marker and its React-key counter, the triage pass's save cursor, and the menu's keyboard-open flag are cursors and markers over a single render path, none deciding async work; a keyed read's, a write's and the subscription's retry budgets live in their machine's context.

Alternatives: the scheduler holding every reported fact (copies of the stream, skew and the edit count beside their owners), or reading owners through input functions (a hand-made subscription, as `SyncStatus` had); a parallel read region during a turn (a second read lifecycle to order against the re-read, which is what the counter did); an `activity` child machine with one state; receipts under the root.

What would change it: a second consumer of the write-start count that needs it reactive; a receipt that must outlive its control; a conversation cost that makes eviction worth its code.
