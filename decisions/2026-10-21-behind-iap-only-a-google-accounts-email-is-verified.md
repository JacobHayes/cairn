# Behind IAP, only Google accounts sign in, and the relay holds a few request slots of its own

- Question: a security review of the `gcp_iap` provider found that it listed the assertion's email as verified for every identity. IAP can also sign in Identity Platform (external) identities: their subject begins with the Identity Platform token issuer and tenant (`securetoken.google.com/<project>/<tenant>:<uid>`) instead of `accounts.google.com:`, and their email comes from that identity's own provider. Google documents that provider's `email_verified` inside the `gcip` claim, and shows it `false` for some providers. Since every request matches the caller to the identity holding their verified emails (H3), an external identity carrying someone's unverified email would have been matched to that person,. The review also asked for a rate limit on the relay's public token endpoint, by way of Cloud Armor, which a deployment need not use. Not part of any brief: this came from the review.
- Call:
  - The email is listed as verified only when the subject is a Google account's (`accounts.google.com:`). An external identity's login then has no verified email, and a login with none is refused (H3), so behind IAP only Google accounts sign in.
  - The relay's endpoints (metadata, registration, authorize, the callback, the token endpoint) hold at most 16 requests in flight, the body read included, and answer the rest 503 with `Retry-After` at once. The token endpoint, registration and the metadata are reachable without IAP, and every request shares the process's 128 slots, so before this a stranger keeping those slots busy (bodies that trickle in for the 10 s deadline, or junk codes Google takes time to refuse) made every signed-in request fail too. Now such a flood stops new MCP sign-ins and refreshes while it lasts, and nothing else. It needs no way to tell callers apart, so no configuration and no Cloud Armor.
- Alternatives:
  - Read `gcip.email_verified` and list an external identity's email when it is true. Rejected for now: it parses a nested JSON-string claim for identities no deployment uses yet. Failing closed costs external identities all access, not only linking.
  - A per-client rate limit keyed on the balancer's `X-Forwarded-For` entry. Rejected: anyone who reaches Cairn without the balancer can forge it, and trusting it only from the balancer's addresses needs configuration or Google's address ranges hard-coded. Per-client limits belong at the edge (a policy such as Cloud Armor), if a deployment wants them.
  - A cap on Cairn's calls to Google alone. Rejected: the form is read before the handler runs, so slow bodies would still hold the process's slots.
- What would change it: a deployment that signs in external identities, or Workforce Identity Federation identities, whose subjects are not `accounts.google.com:` either (read the verified email those carry); or MCP sign-ins hitting the 16.
