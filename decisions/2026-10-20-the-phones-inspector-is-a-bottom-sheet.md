# The phone's inspector is a bottom sheet with a peek, the same sheet as a tablet's

- Question: below 720px the inspector was stacked under the page (a node picked scrolled it into view, and hiding it left a 32px bar), the form the owner doubted and then replaced after a bug: a hidden inspector left a blank area with a bar that looked draggable but was not (phone-inspector-sheet).
- Call: one bottom sheet below 1100px, tablet and phone alike, replacing both the tablet's two-height sheet with its 24px hidden bar and the phone's stacked column. Three heights (peek, half, full) read from CSS, `--sheet-covers` as the one number the workspace, canvas controls, scroll margins and toasts clear. The handle is a real control (pointer and touch drag with snapping, tap, arrow keys); a release below the peek, the hide button and Esc close it; closed, the sheet is a single labelled pill and the page ends above it, so no bar or blank area remains. A node picked opens it at the peek on a phone (half on a tablet keeps the last height). At the peek the pane is inert and a tap on it, or choosing a tab, opens the sheet to half. Esc steps back (assistant, then the open node, then the sheet itself), which on a tablet newly closes a sheet that had no node open. A phone's graph is sized to end at the peek's top, so the peek covers nothing of it. The stacked layout, the scroll-into-view of the column and the 32px bar are deleted.
- Alternatives: keep the stacked column (the owner chose the sheet); a modal sheet that blocks the page (the graph must stay usable at the peek); a library for drag sheets (a hundred lines of pointer events do it, and a dependency would own the snap heights CSS already owns); leaving a handle bar when closed (the owner's bug).
- What would change it: a phone where the peek's 160px takes too much of the window (a landscape phone is the likely case), or a second surface that needs the sheet's content beside the page rather than over it.
