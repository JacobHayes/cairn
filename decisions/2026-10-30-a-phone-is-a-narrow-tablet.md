# A phone's frame is a tablet's with the rail across the top

Question: below 720px, does the page flow as one column the document scrolls, with the graph sized into it, or keep the frame every wider window has?

Call: the frame is the same at every width: the window never scrolls, each region's body is its scroller, and the graph fills what the head leaves. Below 720px the rail is a bar across the top; below 1100px the inspector is a sheet over the workspace's foot, its controls and the fit clear of it. The sheet's smallest height is its head alone (the tabs, the grip, the hide button), with no pane showing. Opening a node sets it at half, so the detail shows below and the card is framed above; it holds its height across a resize or a turn, and the person moves it with the grip. Whether the panes can be reached at the peek is the stylesheet's to say (they are not drawn there), so nothing in the app marks them unreachable by its own reading of the window.

Alternatives: the flowing page (the graph sat in the document's scroller, so a tall head pushed it under the sheet, where its fit had no room and drew the graph at the smallest zoom); a peek showing the top of the pane (a third of a phone's height taken before anything is asked); opening a node at the peek (the detail is then one more tap away).

What would change it: a phone screen whose content cannot live in a region of its own.
