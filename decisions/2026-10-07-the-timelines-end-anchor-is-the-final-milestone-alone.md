# The timeline's end anchor is the final milestone alone; without one the timeline ends after its latest date

- Question (brief 5.4): C13 puts milestones, pins, and due dates on a time axis "with the `final` milestone as the end anchor", but a graph may have no final milestone (A1a allows at most one, so none is valid: the hiring loop has none), a final milestone may have no date yet, and dates may fall after it (the product launch's retrospective is no sooner than five days after the launch).
- Call: the anchor is the final milestone at its effective date and nothing else, as the engine's timeline projection names it (`end`). It is drawn as a solid line down every row, named on the axis, and its row marked as the end; dates after it are still drawn, on a shaded stretch past the end, never cut. With no final milestone in scope, or one with no date yet, there is no anchor: the page says so, and the axis simply ends after the latest date. The axis always spans today and every date, with a two-day margin.
- Alternatives: ending the axis at the final milestone and dropping what lies after it (hides real dates); a stand-in anchor without a final milestone, such as the latest pin or the latest milestone (presents as the end a node the route never declared to be one).
- What would change it: a PRD answer that a journey with no final milestone still has a named end; that rule would then go in the engine's timeline projection, where every host would share it, not in the page.
