# What satisfies dependencies, and what the frontier promises

- Question: D1 has terminal states satisfy dependencies and D1a gives skipped containers the kept-work exception, but neither says whether a `done` container satisfies its dependents after a child is reopened, how force include under a not-relevant container meets the PRD's "the frontier has no actionable descendants", or where auto-reach sits in the order.
- Call: a node's finish is satisfied when it is stored or effectively skipped and all kept, in-scope work beneath it is satisfied; when it is otherwise terminal; when it is a group whose start is satisfied (children, own and inherited gates); or when it is a pending, relevant `auto_reach` milestone whose start is satisfied and whose effective date is today or earlier. Every other instant is satisfied when each pruned gate it waits on is. One sweep in a topological order of the full set's gate edges decides them, auto-reach included, so milestones chain. A `done` container satisfies its dependents whatever was reopened beneath it (that is `stale`, not blocking), and nothing above a not-relevant container waits on what a force include keeps beneath it (2.2's pruned set); so "no actionable descendants" holds through open, in-scope ancestors only, and derive asserts exactly that. `deps_done` is the node's start; blocked is in scope, not closed, and not `deps_done`; actionable additionally needs `relevant` (undecided is not actionable) and not a group.
- Alternatives: a reopened child un-satisfying a `done` parent (a completion would silently stop counting, contradicting D1); a force-included node waited on by the in-scope ancestors above its not-relevant parent (a second pruning rule for containment that 2.2 and 2.3 do not have).
- What would change it: users completing a container while a reopened child is open and expecting its dependents to wait, which would argue for a done-with-open-children state.
