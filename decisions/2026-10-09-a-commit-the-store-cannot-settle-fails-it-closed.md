# A commit the store cannot settle fails it closed, and every open starts from the disk

- Question: the durability testbed's finding (a log fsync fails, Turso keeps the commit visible, a resubmission is acknowledged from it, a crash loses it) was fixed with a "barrier": a commit of its own that synced the log past the failed one, a counter of failures against failures covered, a table for it, and an answer for the failed commit read back from its receipt. The user asked for the simplest rule that never answers from a write that may not be on disk.
- Call (`crates/store-turso/src/durable.rs`): a `COMMIT` that fails other than by a conflict or a constraint (those two fail before the log record is written) leaves the store's state in doubt, so the store fails closed: the failing call is answered failed, every later call errs, `GET /healthz` answers 503, and `cairn serve` stops and exits non-zero (`crates/cairn/src/root.rs` looks at the health once a second), so a supervisor restarts it. A commit or read in flight when the store closes is answered failed, never applied. A resubmission after the restart is answered from its receipt when the commit landed, as H5 already says.
- Every open first drops the cached pages of the database file and of its logical log (Linux: `posix_fadvise` with `POSIX_FADV_DONTNEED`, through `rustix` for a safe call; elsewhere nothing), then opens normally. A failed fsync leaves the record as a clean page in the operating system's cache, so without this a restarted process reads it back as if it were on disk. Migration 8 drops the `log_barrier` table; its machinery (the barrier, the counters, the open-time barrier, the settled answer) is removed. The conformance case for a failure injected mid-commit stays: the store fails closed, and a reopen holds the commit whole or not at all.
- Cost: a transient `EIO`, `ENOSPC`, or `EINTR` on the log sync now costs a restart instead of being ridden out. The server is one process behind a supervisor, and a restart recovers from disk.
- Limits: `DONTNEED` drops clean pages only; Linux may instead drop dirty pages after a failed fsync and report the next one successful, which no cache eviction repairs (the earlier record's limit, unchanged). Patina models `posix_fadvise` as accepting every advice and changing nothing, so the durability testbed cannot show the eviction itself; it shows the store failing closed and the run recovering after a reopen.
- Alternatives: the barrier ([`2026-10-07-the-log-sync-fix-nothing-is-answered-from-a-write-until.md`](2026-10-07-the-log-sync-fix-nothing-is-answered-from-a-write-until.md), superseded by this): it rode out transient errors, at the price of a table, counters, a lock, a second failure mode (Busy while settling), and an open that wrote. Rolling the record back in Turso: the upstream bug (an error from `COMMIT` while the row stays committed and visible) is reproduced in a draft issue with a runnable reproduction, `/tmp/cairn/turso-mre/README.md` on the build machine, not yet filed. Reopening in process instead of exiting: Turso cannot open again reliably in a process whose open failed (2026-10-07-turso-an-open-under-injected-i-o-faults-panics-or-fails.md).
- What would change it: Turso rolling back a commit whose log sync failed (then a failed `COMMIT` means not committed and nothing needs to close); evidence that transient sync errors are common enough that restarts hurt.
