# A proposal's after is the engine's, derived beside its destination

Question: a proposal is shown as a layer over its destination, so the views need the destination as it would be after the proposal, with states, dates, frontier and rank. Where does that after come from?

Call: from the engine, never assembled or replayed in the page. The engine derives the after against the after deployment (`destination_after`), so a proposal that creates an identity and assigns it is valid and a merge moves emails and aliases as apply would. For an existing journey, the worker derives the after over the journey's held derivation and holds it in a slot of its own per preview, so releasing a preview never frees the journey's derivation or another preview's, and a preview whose source derivation was replaced is refused. For other destinations the host's preview answer carries the after. A candidate that breaks a rule has no after: the views draw the destination as it is, with the touched nodes marked.

Alternatives: a union of the before and the removed nodes (no states or dates, and it can break invariants); replaying mutations in TypeScript (wrong for merges and new identities); one derivation per listed proposal.

What would change it: an after too large to hold beside its destination's derivation.
