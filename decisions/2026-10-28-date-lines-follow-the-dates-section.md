# Date lines are drawn while the Dates section is open

Question: the date lines for the selected node appeared on every selection, and the owner found the arrows confusing without the words beside them. The inspector had two sections, Dates and Linked dates, so the lines and what they said lived apart. When should the lines show, and where do the words live? (From the graph-overview work.)

Call: the node's dates are one Dates section: the due date, then the linked dates (each row naming the other node and the relationship), then earliest start, latest start and slack, then the shortfall explanation. The date lines, the focus kept on their far ends and Frame's "and its linked dates" are drawn only while that section is open and the inspector is showing node detail, for a single selected node. The section uses the existing per-kind fold (the `dates` key, open by default on a shortfall or when overdue), so someone who leaves it open sees the lines for each node they select with the explanation beside them; folding it, selecting several nodes, hiding the inspector, showing another tab or a sheet at its peek removes the lines. Scrolling the section out of sight does not: the panel opens at its top on each selection, so tracking the scroll would hide the lines of a section left open below a long description, against the point of remembering the fold. The authored date fields of a form and the rules of a published route version are a separate section named Date rules, so a node has one Dates section in every inspector. Pointing is by relationship, not by the other node, and hover and focus are held apart. Pointing at a linked-date row (hover or keyboard focus) keeps the lines standing for that row in ink and steps the others back to a faded line's level. The separate `linked-dates` fold key is gone, and a route's inspector has the same section with its linked dates. This supersedes "there is no show or hide setting" in the earlier decision on date relationships: the section's fold is the switch, with no setting of its own.

Alternatives: a toggle on the canvas (a second control with one more place to look, and not remembered with the inspector); keeping two sections (the words stay apart from the lines they explain); drawing at rest (unreadable at scale, as the earlier decision found); fading rather than dropping the lines when folded (the picture stays busy and the arrows stay unexplained).

What would change it: people who want lines with the section folded, which would call for a canvas control after all.
