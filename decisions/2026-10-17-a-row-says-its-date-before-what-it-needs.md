# A row says its date before what it needs

Question: a row carries one fact. When a node is late, near its date, behind, or waiting on something, which does it say?

Call: the first that applies, in order of weight: conditional; late; behind; a start or due date within the next week (skipped when the list is sorted by a signal whose reason is the point of the list); what it needs; a dated decision outside the soon window; then the sort's reason. Late and behind take error ink, soon takes warning ink.

Alternatives: the sort's reason first (it hides that a node is late behind "unlocks 4"); several facts per row (crowds the row, and one still has to lead).

What would change it: readers finding the second fact elsewhere, or a product rule ranking dates and needs differently.
