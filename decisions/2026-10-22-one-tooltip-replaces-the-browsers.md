# One tooltip replaces the browser's

Question: the app had two tooltips side by side: the browser's own, from a native `title` on about 40 controls, cards and chips (shown after a long, uncontrollable delay, unstyled, never on keyboard focus, never on touch), and the graph line's own chip, which was prompt and styled and looked far better. The ban on custom tooltips and first-run hints came in with a rewrite of the visual language and had no record of its own. Should the app keep both, and what does it do with the ban? (Work: the tooltip unit, from the owner's request.)

Call: one tooltip component for the whole app, grown from the line's chip and the keys popover's placement, and no native `title` anywhere (a browser test fails any page that carries one, including one a library draws). The ban on custom tooltips and the clause against first-run hints are gone from the visual language, replaced by the goal they stood in for: guidance appears when someone needs it, can be dismissed, and does not come back once seen.

- Kinds. A tip describes (what pressing a control does, what a badge counts, why a control is held), names (shows the accessible name of an icon-only control, hidden from assistive technology), or shows a text whole only while it is cut short (also hidden from assistive technology: the text is in the page). A tip that would repeat the words or the name beside it is not drawn; a list longer than a few lines belongs in the inspector.
- Held controls. A control that cannot act now but has a reason is `aria-disabled`, stays focusable, and says why in its tip; a press does nothing but show it. A control with no reason to give stays plainly `disabled`. A held menu item is reached by the arrow keys so its reason can be read.
- Timing. A tip shows 300ms after the pointer rests (a move restarts the rest), at once while another has just shown (500ms), and at once on keyboard focus. It stays while the pointer is on it (the leave beat is the sync popover's, one shared constant). Hover and focus are kept apart: a tip opened by focus stays with the focus. Esc only dismisses it; a second Esc does what Esc usually does. Nothing opens while a pointer button is held.
- Touch and keyboard. A fact whose only place is a tip (a date's origin, a badge's meaning, the rest of a list) is a toggletip: it takes the focus, Enter, Space or a tap shows it, and a tap does nothing else, so a tap inside a clickable row does not navigate. Its words carry a dotted underline. A graph line that opens no card shows its words on a tap; a focused line shows them at once, and they are the line's accessible name.
- Placement and layering. One pure placement function (`place()`) puts any floating box beside an anchor, flips it when the other side has more room and slides it inside the window; the tooltip, the keys preview, the sync popover, the context menu and the dropdown menus all call it. A tip is a `popover="manual"` element beside its trigger, so it sits in the browser's top layer: above modals, outside every scroller and canvas transform, with no portal and no z-index, and showing it never re-renders the trigger. The layer owns one timing machine and delegated document listeners. `place()` also positions the tour's anchored callout if the tour takes that form.
- The keys hint. It listed a journey's keys on every screen though only journey and route pages bind any, and a press did nothing. It is now drawn only where the page binds keys, a press opens that page's key sheet, and the hover preview is the same table drawn as a popover through the shared component.
- Removed. The graph's own chip and its style, the keys popover's placement code, the zoom buttons' native titles (the app draws its own three), the card-wide tip that restated the status chip, the owner and the head on every pass of the pointer (what the card does not say is a line only a screen reader reads), the checklist tip on a container's progress, and the tips that repeated their label.

Alternatives: keep `title` and restyle the edge chip alone (the browser's delay and look cannot be changed, and it has no keyboard or touch path); a positioning library such as Floating UI (the window's edges are the only bounds, and flip plus slide is about 30 lines that the keys popover and the context menu already did half of each); per-trigger React state (every card would re-render on hover, which the canvas's rule forbids); a portal per tip (the Popover API already gives the top layer, and is baseline in the browsers the tokens already require); letting a hover tip's Esc fall through to the selection (one key doing two things changes the task under someone reading).

What would change it: a browser floor below the Popover API (the tips would need a portal and a z-index); a tip that has to hold a control (it would become a popover, as the sync dot's is); the tour taking the overlay form, which reuses `place()` and adds a notch to its result.
