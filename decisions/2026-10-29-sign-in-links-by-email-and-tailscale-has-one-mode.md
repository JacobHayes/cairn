# Sign-in has no link step, no per-provider auto-link, and no direct Tailscale mode

- Question: the five sign-in providers took about 9.4k lines with their tests; what can go without losing something a deployment uses?
- Call: a new login whose verified email another user's login holds joins that user; otherwise it creates a user. The rule is one small path in the accounts code, always on, with no setting; emails held by two users link to neither. The browser-linking flow (sign in with another provider while signed in) and the per-provider `auto_link` setting are gone. A session cookie that no longer names a live session is ignored and cleared by the challenge, not refused. The Tailscale provider has one mode, proxy, with a required `trusted_proxies`; the direct mode (asking tailscaled whois over its socket) and the `mode` and `socket` settings are gone, and the provider no longer needs to know the listener. The authorize-request checks are shared by the built-in OAuth server and the IAP relay.
- Alternatives: keeping the link step for deployments with several providers (the email rule covers the common case); keeping direct mode for tailnet-only hosts (no deployment runs it, and `tailscale serve` plus `trusted_proxies = ["127.0.0.1"]` covers the same host); a per-provider switch for matching by email (the rule is safe only for emails the issuer verified, which every provider already lists alone).
- What would change it: a deployment that needs one user across logins whose emails differ, which would call for an explicit link step again.
