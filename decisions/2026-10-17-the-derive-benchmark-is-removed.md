# The derive benchmark is removed

- Question (from cleaning up proof scripts nothing runs): ARCHITECTURE's Date network section asked for a benchmark of derive at the limits, recording elapsed time and peak memory natively and in the browser's wasm, "reported, not gated". Its pieces were the 4.5 proof's `prove.sh`, `web/wasm/proof/bench.ts`, the `--bench` flag of the `cairn-wasm-cases` binary, the engine's `date_benchmark` example, and the elapsed times the projection cost test printed. No mise task, check step or CI job ran any of them; they ran only when someone remembered to. Should they stay?
- Call: delete them and the ARCHITECTURE sentence. A reported benchmark that nothing runs is not a check: no change can fail it, and its figures (taken once, on a loaded host) go stale silently. The guard at the limits is the cost tests, which count operations rather than time and run in the full check before every push: the date network's (`cost_dates.rs`) and the projections' (`cost_projections.rs`, which keeps its bound assertions and loses only its printed timings). The `cairn-wasm-cases` binary and its limits and 2,000-node groups stay: the browser host's agreement tests read them. The 4.5 proof keeps its recorded table as the record of what that milestone measured; the earlier brief and decision files that mention the benchmark are history and stay as written.
- Alternatives: keep the scripts as an on-demand tool (code that rots unexercised, and an ARCHITECTURE promise nobody keeps); gate on wall-clock time in the check (flaky on a shared, loaded host, which is why the cost tests count operations).
- What would change it: a responsiveness problem at the limits, such as derive or a projection that is within its operation budget but slow in the browser. Then a benchmark comes back as a step something runs, with the figure it is meant to keep under.
