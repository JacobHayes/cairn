# The phone's graph is in the page, with no preview and no full-screen map

- Question: below 720px a graph was an inert preview with an "Open map" button, and the map opened as a fixed full-screen layer that covered the page's header (so Apply, Save the edits, the drawing banner and the stale-refresh notice had to be re-carried into it) with the inspector as a sheet over it. What does the phone's graph do instead (phone-layout)?
- Call: the canvas is drawn in the page like on any other width, live, at the window's height less 280px (never under 360px), so the page's head stays above it and the inspector's head peeks under it. The peek is the page's own scroll handle: a swipe on the canvas pans it, a swipe on the head or the peek scrolls the page, and picking a node already scrolls the stacked inspector into view. The inspector stays stacked under the page as it is today (its form on a phone, a bottom sheet with a peek or the stacked column, is a separate open question and is not decided here); it gains the same single hide control as every width, folding to a 32px bar in the page. DESIGN's Responsive rule is rewritten to say so, and the code that served the preview is deleted: the map component, the `map=1` address parameter and its carrying through view switches, the map's band and banner carrying, the inspector-as-sheet-over-the-map layout, and the `inert` and `inScroller` modes of the canvas.
- Alternatives: keep the preview and move the controls into the map layer (what the preview cost: a tap to reach the graph, and every control the page offers needing a copy in the layer); a graph that fills the window with no peek (a swipe anywhere on it pans, so the page cannot be scrolled from it); a bottom-sheet inspector (the owner's open question).
- What would change it: the owner's answer on the inspector's form on a phone (a sheet would take the peek's place and make the canvas fill the window), or a phone user who cannot find the way off the canvas to the page.
