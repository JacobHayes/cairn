# Zoomed-out cards stay whole, and done work fades

Question (from the graph canvas zoom review): zoomed out, cards swapped their content for a title and a state glyph, so a title was readable but the state was a symbol to learn, and done work was a solid green fill that competed with the work still to do. What should a zoomed-out graph show?

Call: no title-only level. A card is always drawn whole and only scales, so at fit its text is unreadable, by design: the graph shows its shape, and the stages are what you read. An open container's head, below the near zoom, is simplified to its title (counter-scaled to 13px on screen, shrunk between whole words before it is ever cut) with a stage's state chip at the right. Opacity is one hierarchy, 1 open and in focus, 0.5 done, 0.3 out of focus, and the lowest applies, never their product. Done cards take the neutral card fill and muted text with the green done chip, whose dot is a check. A finished list row keeps its pale green fill.

Alternatives: keep a mid-zoom title level (it was a second look to maintain for text that is still small); fade done work with the fill kept (green and faded read as two states); multiply the opacities (a done card out of focus would nearly vanish); fade list rows too (not asked, and rows are read at full size).

What would change it: people losing track of what a card is at fit, which would bring back a title on leaf cards at a fixed screen size.
