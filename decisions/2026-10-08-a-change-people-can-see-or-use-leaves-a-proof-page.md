# A change people can see or use leaves a proof page

- Question: every brief ended with a proof page under `briefs/proof/<brief id>/`. Every brief has now landed and work arrives as features, fixes, refactors, test and CI changes, and documents, none of them a brief. Which of those still leave a proof, and where does it go (rewriting `AGENTS.md` as the guide for any agent)?
- Call: a change someone can see or use, a screen or flow, an endpoint, a command or setting, or a value Cairn derives, leaves a proof page in the brief proofs' format, committed with the work. A fix that restores intended behaviour, a refactor, tests, CI, and documents leave none: the regression test, the planted failure in the commit body, and the commit message carry the evidence, and a page would only restate them. The page goes in `briefs/proof/YYYY-MM-DD-<short-slug>/README.md`, named like a decision record, so every proof sits in one directory, the brief proofs sort first by id, and later ones by date. Earlier proofs stay as they are: they record what their milestone shipped, and a later change that alters what one shows leaves its own page rather than rewriting history.
- Alternatives: a proof for every change (a page for a CI fix or a refactor shows nothing a person can look at, and costs a page per commit); no proofs after the briefs (a new screen or endpoint would land with nothing a person can take in at a glance); a top-level `proof/` directory (a second home for the same kind of page, and one more entry at the root); updating the brief's own proof when a later change alters it (the brief's page would no longer show what that milestone shipped, and its media would need regenerating against code the brief never saw).
- What would change it: proofs piling up for small visible tweaks with little to show, which would argue for a size threshold; a reader who wants the current state of a screen rather than its history, which would argue for a living page per screen.
