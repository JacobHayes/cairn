# The Summary is the journey's front page

Question (from redesigning the journey Summary, which the owner found uninspiring): what does the page someone opens a journey on say first, in what order, and from which values?

Call: the Summary answers, in this order, where the journey stands (what is left in scope, the counts by display state, the final milestone), what is wrong (overdue, short of days, stale, each with every reason, and the stalled diagnostic), what to do (what waits on the viewer, the decisions that can be made now, the rest of the acting frontier, the decisions still waiting with what each waits on), and the context (upcoming milestones, recently finished work, the description, notes and lineage). It reads only values Cairn already derives (the status summary, the next list, mine, the derivation's stalled diagnostic and stored finish dates): no engine concept, read or API is added.

- One action row per node across the action panels: the sets are claimed in order (the viewer's, decisions to make now, the rest of the frontier, decisions waiting) before any cap shows five rows, so a node never appears twice and a panel's count is its claimed set.
- "Waiting on you" is work the viewer owns (owner participation, through merged entities), not any participation. The "mine" projection (E4) is wider, since it includes informed participants; this page asks who holds the pen.
- The headline is what is left. The meter is what is finished or skipped of what is in scope (the summary's `remaining`), while the counts by display state say "done" exactly, so a skipped container moves the meter and not the done count.
- Only the `final` milestone is ever called the end (C13). With none, the page says "Next milestone" from the earliest upcoming one. A shortfall shown beside a milestone is that milestone's own. A past date is "late", never a completion date; a reached milestone says "reached".
- Recently finished uses the recorded finish date (F2) and shows no answer text, because a revised answer does not move the finish date and pairing them could misstate history.
- Blocked work is listed and says what it waits for: a folded Blocked panel names, for each blocked node that is not a container and not already on an action list, what holds it (its first unblocker with a count of the rest, from the same unblockers the waiting decisions use), and an In progress panel lists active work nothing is asked of (a container says how much inside it is still open). What is not built is a count of everything *behind* each blocked node (its downstream gates): the tab's explanations cut `gravity_from` to `explanation_entry_count_max` entries and leave out zero-weight nodes, so they cannot give exact counts. A node's own blockers are exact, so those are shown.
- A completed or archived journey says so in the lead, with how much it left unfinished, calls its figure "unfinished" and heads its lists "Left unfinished" and "Decisions left open": completion may legitimately leave work, and the page must not read as a queue.
- Milestones are a compact list of titles and dates, leaving out the one the lead already names, not node rows again; the lead's counts-as-links sentence moves to the foot of Needs attention, so the same facts are not said in three places.
- A shortfall is said as what it means for the node ("Needs 2 more days than its chain allows"; "Reached 2 days later than its chain allows" for finished work), with a link to its chain, never as a bare "2 days short". A date is its nearness then its calendar date, and where it comes from (pinned or derived) is on hover.
- A decision that sets the final milestone's date says so on its row, because answering it is what gives the journey an end.
- Every panel on the page has DESIGN's panel frame (the node panels and folds gain the corner ticks, border and shadow the lead and About already had), and its heading is DESIGN's Section title (Space Grotesk 1.25rem / 700 uppercase) with the count in mono. The small bold label the node panels had was neither the panel band label nor a section title.
- DESIGN's done-row opacity (0.5) conflicts with its 4.5:1 contrast floor for the text of rows the page is read for. DESIGN now says a list read for its finished work (Recently finished, finished nodes under Needs attention) keeps its done rows at full strength. Done rows elsewhere keep the fade: that call is out of this change.
- Completion is not suggested for a journey with no nodes: B11's "every relevant or undecided node satisfies dependencies" is read as needing at least one node, so an empty journey no longer offers to complete itself.
- An archived journey's Summary is read-only: no row actions, no note edits, no upgrade, no planning invitation (A19).
- The status colours are set as text on this page (late and short in error ink, near dates in warning ink, the done chip's word), and the old tokens fell below DESIGN's own 4.5:1 floor for text (warning on paper 4.28:1, and on dark paper error 2.9:1, warning 3.4:1, success 2.9:1). The tokens are corrected centrally, a lighter step of each in the dark theme and a darker warning on paper, and `tokens.test.ts` now holds them to the floor, so every screen that sets them as text benefits. DESIGN's "same in both themes" line for status colours is replaced accordingly.
- A finished node's shortfall is said as how far off its chain it is ("Reached 2 days off what its chain allows"), never as late: a recorded date can break a not-before chain by being too early. An unreachable-end warning on a stalled journey is not made once the final milestone is reached.
- On a narrow list the owner is a line of its own, as wide as the title, with the actions under it on a touch screen and over the row's corner where a hover reveals them, so neither squeezes the name nor holds an empty gap while hidden.

Smaller calls (the sentence form of dates, Print in `⋯`, five rows before Show all, the 960px reflow and the 1280px measure, owners always visible, expansion not kept across navigation) are in the commit message.

Alternatives: an engine field on the status summary that counts what each blocked node waits behind, computed from blocking and covered by a property test, so agents get it too; showing every participation under "Waiting on you"; a milestone strip on the Summary (the Timeline already draws it).

What would change it: the owner or an agent asking for what blocked work waits behind (then the engine field); evidence that people want informed participation on this page.
