# Screens are approachable by default

Question (from the web redesign): after seeing the redesign, the user found it too dense: Cairn must feel friendly and approachable, leaning harder on progressive disclosure. Graticule's principle was "dense, but organised".

Call: DESIGN gains "Approachable by default: density and progressive disclosure", which wins over denser defaults. A row or card shows its title, its status, and at most one secondary fact; rows are roomier (about 64px for two lines) and text 15-16px; mono uppercase is kept for small labels and status chips, with buttons, menus, toolbar text, and inspector headings in sentence case; dates and figures read as words ("Due in 3 days"); the journey header is one line, the toolbar holds the tabs, Decisions, one Filter button (Mine inside it), search, and the switcher; the strip shows only the sync chip and "? Keys"; inspector sections are folded rows with a name and a count; graph cards show a body line only for a decided decision's answer and an owner only when it is the viewer or missing; rank tags mark the top three. The ruling, corner ticks, and hairlines stay.

Alternatives: keeping the dense layout with a "compact" toggle (a knob, against Limits, not knobs, and the default is what most people see).

What would change it: daily users asking for more at a glance on a specific surface, which would earn that surface one more visible fact, not a global density switch.
